*Desk research (done online, without talking to anyone yet). Malaysia only, education only.
Assembled 2026-10-15 from six research write-ups, called "streams", one per business type or
topic.*

**How to read it.** About 30 minutes for sections 1–10. Short on time: read sections 1, 8 and 9

- **Unknown**: searched for and not found.

## 1. One-page summary

**What this is.** On 15 Oct you floated a pivot (a big change of direction): sell the university
research to education businesses instead of to families at RM50. This pack maps those businesses
so you know **who to talk to and what to ask**. It cannot tell you whether anyone would pay. Only
conversations can, so the pack ends in a ranked contact list (section 8) and an interview script
(section 9), not a verdict.

**The six findings that matter most**

1. **"Who pays them" splits the market in two.** Agents (companies that place students at
    universities abroad), portals (websites that match students to courses), some pre-university
    colleges and most "free" software are paid by universities, through commission (a fee the
    university pays per enrolled student). An honest, any-university list works against their
    income.

    Paid consultants (advisers that families pay directly for application help), international
    schools and scholarship bodies are paid by families, schools or sponsors. For them, an honest
    list matches what their own customer wants [Known for who pays each type; Guess for the
    effect].

2. **The list is built by busy, often junior people.**

    - Agent and portal counsellors start on RM3,000–4,500 a month. They have enrolment targets (a
      set number of students they must sign up) and answer students' messages at night [Known,
      job ads].
    - One international-school counsellor can be responsible for a 300-student sixth form (the
      last two years of a British-style school, ages 16–18), plus teaching and pastoral work
      (looking after students' wellbeing) [Known, KTJ job description].
    - Strategists (the senior advisers who plan each student's applications) at the biggest paid
      consultancy, Crimson Education, report 40–50 students each [Known, but from Glassdoor (a
      website where employees review their employers), by staff outside Malaysia].

3. **Nobody in the market shows the total cost of the whole degree in ringgit.** Not the agents,
    the consultants, the universities' own counsellors or the software (Cialfo, BridgeU, Unifrog,
    ApplyBoard: online platforms that schools and agents use to manage applications). HELP and INTI
    colleges give rough cost estimates, and Unifrog shows tuition only [Known for every page
    opened]. This is the clearest gap the product fills.

4. **Prices already paid for similar tools are low.** Software that schools already buy costs
    about RM19 to RM137 per student, or RM3,300–4,100 a year for a school licence (one yearly fee
    for the whole school) [Known prices; RM is an estimate]. That is close to your RM50, not far
    above it. A higher B2B (business-to-business) price would have to come from **doing the work
    for them** (a service), not from the list itself [Guess].

5. **Handling a client's student data makes you a "data processor" under PDPA (the Personal
    Data Protection Act), and since 2025 that role has its own legal duties.** A data processor is
    a company that handles personal data on another company's behalf. The duties are manageable at
    your size, but the client must accept that student data goes through overseas services
    (Claude, the AI model, and Firecrawl, a web-scraping service) [Known for the law; Guess for how
    it applies to you].

6. **The evidence of problems comes from job ads, not from customers.** No Malaysian forum or
    review evidence was found in any stream. Which problems these businesses would pay to fix
    (their "pain points") is still unconfirmed.

**Recommendation (Guess, to be tested).** Talk first to **international-school university
counsellors** (school staff who help students apply to universities) and **small paid
consultancies**. Both are paid by families, both are short of time, and neither loses money when a
list is honest. Use the **Sunway College placement-office chat**, which is already planned, as the
first conversation because it costs nothing to set up. Treat agents and private universities as
people to *learn from*, not to sell to.

> **Before you pitch any business, decide this:** an agent, a college or a university will probably
> ask for a version filtered to their partner universities, or with the "reasons not to go" taken
> out. That would break the product's main promise. Decide in advance whether you would accept
> it, not during a meeting.

## 2. B2B primer

B2B means **business-to-business**: you sell to an organisation, not to a family. It works
differently from selling RM50 lists, in ways that change what you ask in an interview.

| Term | Plain meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer vs user** | The person who signs (a principal, a founder, a procurement office: the department that handles purchases) is often not the person who uses the product (a counsellor) | Get the user to want it, then find out who signs. Ask "who approved the last tool you bought?" |
| **Payer vs customer** | Who hands over the money may not be the person being served. Agents serve students, but universities pay them | This is the conflict test: whoever pays decides what "a good list" means |
| **Sales cycle** | Time from first meeting to payment | One meeting at a small agency or consultancy. Months at a university group or a government body [Guess] |
| **Procurement** | An organisation's formal buying process: forms, approved-supplier lists, quotations (formal written price offers) | Large groups and MARA have one. It can block a sole trader (a one-person business) outright (section 6) |
| **Pilot** | A small, time-limited trial, ideally paid, before a bigger deal | Your first B2B offer would be a pilot, e.g. 10 students in one cohort (year group) |
| **Commitment** | Something the buyer gives up to move forward: time, reputation, money or data | The only real signal in an interview. Compliments aren't commitments (section 9) |
| **Done-for-you service** | You do the work and hand over the result | What you can actually deliver today, since the product is you running the pipeline (the step-by-step research process, done per student) |
| **White-label** | Your work sold under the client's brand, with your name off it | Consultants may want this. It hides who did the research |
| **Licence / subscription** | A yearly fee for access, flat or per student | How schools already buy Cialfo, Unifrog and similar platforms |
| **Per-seat** | A price per staff user per month | How agent CRMs (customer-tracking software) are priced |
| **Retainer** | A fixed monthly fee for ongoing work, whatever the volume | Steady income for you; the client pays even in quiet months, so it is hard to win early |
| **Churn** | Customers who stop paying, usually at renewal | Schools renew yearly and do switch vendors (suppliers); Epsom appears to have |
| **Lead / conversion** | A lead is an interested student's contact details; conversion is the share who enrol | Universities and agents are measured on this. It shows what they value |
| **Commission** | A fee the university pays an agent per enrolled student | The core conflict with an any-university list |
| **Data controller / processor** | The controller decides why student data is used; the processor handles it only on the controller's instructions | In every B2B deal you'd be the processor (section 6) |

**How the money flows** [Known for each payment, from streams 1–4]:

- Families pay universities (tuition).
- Universities pay agents, portals and aggregator platforms (websites listing many universities'
  courses in one place) per student.
- Universities pay software firms such as BridgeU and Studyportals for leads and visibility
  (being shown to students).
- Families pay consultants and schools directly.
- Sponsors pay universities and negotiate discounts (MARA gets up to 30% off at some Australian
  universities).

Every payment that comes from a university carries the conflict.

## 4. One profile per buyer type

### 4.1 Agents and free portals

**What they do.** Two kinds. **Study-abroad agents** (IDP, MABECS, AECC, SI-UK, AUG, JM Education
and many smaller firms) mainly send students to the UK and Australia from walk-in offices.
**Online portals** (Uni Enrol, EduAdvisor, EasyUni) mainly send students to Malaysian private
colleges and now do study-abroad too [Known].

**Who pays.** The institution, in almost every case. MABECS: "Our fees are covered by partner
universities". Uni Enrol: "the university pays a service fee". IDP's own financial reports say its
placement count "does not include students placed into non-clients for which we do not receive a
commission" [Known]. Some agents sell paid extras to families: SI-UK's "Premium Application
Service" and AECC's "Premium Counselling", with no prices shown [Known from SI-UK snippet and the
AECC page].

**Key numbers.**

- IDP (the only agent that publishes its financial results) earned A$367.0m (367 million
  Australian dollars) from 70,076 placements (students it placed at universities) in FY25, its
  financial year 2025. That is about A$5,237, or roughly **RM15,200, per placed student** [Known;
  RM estimate]. Malaysia isn't reported separately.
- IDP's placements fell 29% in FY25 and 27% in FY26, and it cut about 1,250 jobs [Known].
- UK commission averaged £1,659–3,529 per student at four universities in 2021/22, with a
  "standard 10%" and large agents reportedly on 20%+ [Known].
- Malaysian institutions that publish a rate pay a flat **RM1,000–2,000** per student (MEDIU 2016,
  USIM RM1,500 in two RM750 instalments, UTHM RM2,000) [Known].
- Uni Enrol said in 2020 that it earns 10% of first-year tuition [Known, from a sponsored
  article, i.e. one the company paid to have published].

**How they build a list.** IDP counsellors use an "AI-enabled Recommendation Engine" (86% of them
use it). Uni Enrol has its own matching over 39,000+ courses. EduAdvisor consultants "research
student questions and confirm information with university representatives" by hand [Known].
Whether IDP's engine recommends only partners is Unknown, but likely, since non-partners earn
nothing [Guess].

**Pain (from job ads).** "A high conversion rate into applications and enrollments", "willing to
answer priority messages at night from students", overtime "after exam results", fresh graduates
welcome [Known, Uni Enrol and EduAdvisor ads, Oct 2026]. Staff are new and learn thousands of
courses on the job [Guess, from the little experience the ads ask for].

**Fit.** Direct conflict. A list that ranks a non-partner (a university that doesn't pay the
agent) first takes money from the agent. Possible openings, all Guess:

- A research tool used by junior staff behind the scenes, never shown to families under the
  agent's brand.
- Students heading somewhere the agent doesn't cover.
- Reach/Match/Safety sorting (hard to get into / realistic / very likely) to cut drop-outs.
  Some UK universities take back the commission when a student drops out early (the University
  of East Anglia does, Known).

**What they do.** Three different businesses [Known]:

- **(a) Crimson Education**, a global premium firm. It sells to Malaysian families from its
  Singapore team, and no KL office was confirmed.
- **(b) Small boutiques (small specialist firms) for KL international-school students** aiming at top US and UK
  universities: PrepWorks (KL-founded, 12 staff), Axiom Learning (Mont Kiara), TigerCampus
  (online tutoring plus counselling), and foreign firms selling into KL (Ivy Summit, InGenius
  Prep, True North).
- **(c) Hybrid "paid agents" such as Inquota**, which charge a fee and also list partner
  universities.

**Who pays.** The family, directly. No type (a) or (b) firm mentions university commission
[Known that none mentions it; whether none takes any is Unknown].

**Key numbers** [Known prices; RM is an estimate]:

- Inquota: from RM2,000 for "full application support".
- TigerCampus: RM70–280 an hour.
- Crimson: S$10,000–30,000 (Singapore dollars) a year in Singapore, about RM31,900–95,700. No
  Malaysian price is published, so this is the nearest stand-in; don't quote it as Crimson
  Malaysia's price.
- Ivy Summit, which names Alice Smith, ISKL and Garden International as schools it serves:
  US$9,500 for a 10-school package (up to 35 hours) up to US$50,000 for its top "VIP" package,
  about RM38,400–202,000.
- The top end of the market is small: Cambridge got 263 applications from Malaysia in 2024 and
  admitted 22.

**How they build a list.** One senior person does it. Crimson's "Strategy Consultant" builds "lists
of best fit schools of varied competition", which is Reach/Match/Safety in other words [Known,
job ad]. The list is one task inside a multi-year package that is mostly essays, activities and
test prep. Prices go up with the number of schools (Ivy Summit, Inquota, Crimson) [Known]. No firm
says how long a list takes [Unknown].

**Pain.** None of this evidence is Malaysian:

- Former Crimson strategists: "40+ student cases per strategist. Feels like an assembly line",
  pay of US$60–70k "even when they come with YEARS of experience", "rude parents" [Known,
  Glassdoor].
- No consultant page shows the total degree cost in any currency [Known for the pages opened].
- Customer reviews of Crimson are mostly positive [Known, Trustpilot, a customer-review
  website]. The problems are with staff workload and price, not obviously with clients.

**Fit.** The best money fit of any business type: the family pays, so an honest list serves the
firm's own customer [Guess]. A strategist with 40–50 students has no time to verify fees, work out
the ringgit total or check MQA recognition (whether Malaysia's qualifications agency accepts the
degree), and that is exactly what you do [Guess]. The risks:

- The list helps justify a RM30,000+ package, so they may only buy it white-labelled (sold
  under their own brand, with your name off it).
- Elite US/UK firms may see a Malaysia-plus-anywhere cost list as off-target.
- Lower-priced firms (Inquota, TigerCampus) may value bought-in research more, because they can't
  afford research hours at their prices [Guess].

### 4.3 Pre-university college placement offices

**What they do.** Colleges run in-house offices that help their own A-Level, foundation and diploma
students apply onward [Known]:

- Taylor's University Placement Services: "each student is assigned to one of the UPS advisors".
- Sunway's International Office University Placement gives "advice on which universities fit best".
- HELP's Student Placement Centre covers "estimated costs of tuition and living" across 44+
  partners.
- INTI's University Placement Office keeps a library of "tuition fees, cost of living" and 150+
  credit-transfer tables (lists of which completed subjects count towards a degree elsewhere).
- MCKL has a smaller placement unit.

**Who pays.** The college, from tuition. A second income stream is possible [Guess, from the
agent-list entries below]:

- "Taylor's Malaysia Sdn Bhd" (the company's registered name) is on the University of
  Liverpool's approved-agent list [Known].
- INTI appears on QUT's (Queensland University of Technology's) "other agents" list [Known,
  snippet only].
- HELP waives application fees for applications made through its centre [Known].

**Pain.** Taylor's advisor job ads (expired, snippets only) speak of "KPIs (key performance
indicators: the targets staff are measured on) related to placing all students to the
universities of their choice". Each advisor acts as a "country custodian", responsible for one
country. That suggests nobody's job is to compare countries on cost side by side [Guess]. No
college publishes how many advisors it has [Unknown].

**Fit.** Mixed:

- **In favour:** placement results are their marketing, and HELP and INTI already hand out cost
  estimates [Known].
- **Against:** Taylor's, Sunway, HELP and INTI each own a university that competes for the same
  students. Taylor's pays bursaries (discounts) of up to RM8,000 to its own pre-U students who
  stay on for a Taylor's degree [Known].
- **Lowest-conflict corner: transfer and American-degree programmes** (start a degree in Malaysia,
  finish it abroad), where the student is leaving anyway. Sunway's School of American Education runs a placement office with workshops
  on "selecting suitable universities", though Arizona State is its "exclusive partner" [Known].

### 4.4 International-school university counsellors

**What they do.** 348 international schools teach 111,185 students in Malaysia, about 67% of them
Malaysian [Known; ISC Research figures for July 2024, reported by Asia News Network]. Larger schools
have a university counsellor or a small team. ISKL pictures seven people, including an educational
psychologist, and reported 550+ offers for its Class of 2026, 37% of them in the US [Known].

**Who pays.** Parents, through fees. The counsellor is paid by the school, not by universities
[Known for fee-funded schools; Guess that no university money reaches them].

**Software already in use** [Known]:

| Platform | Malaysian schools using it |
|---|---|
| Cialfo | Alice Smith, ISKL, IGBIS |
| BridgeU | King Henry VIII College |
| Unifrog | Epsom, UCSI International, Straits Rawang, Kinabalu International (2020 evidence) |
| MaiaLearning | Oasis International |
| Uni Enrol's Pathway Match | Cempaka |

Alice Smith probably uses both Unifrog (named on its careers page) and Cialfo (named in a 2023
blog post) [Known for each page].

**Pain.** One person, many jobs [Known]:

- KTJ's single "designated US counsellor" for a 300-student sixth form must also tutor, teach
  personal and social education, write references (recommendation letters), run university fairs and "keep thorough and accurate
  records of student applications, outcomes and final destinations".
- IGBIS combines wellbeing counselling and college counselling in one role, asking for
  "impartial, data-based options' counsel".
- Pay at the lower end is RM2,900–4,000 a month.
- Alice Smith's counsellor writes that AI "can only suggest", so counsellors still check the
  tool's list by hand.

**Fit.** The best fit on paper [Guess from Known facts]:

- No money conflict.
- "Impartial" advice is written into the job.
- These schools already have a budget line (money set aside each year) for guidance software.

The catches:

- Free tools paid for by universities (BridgeU, Uni Enrol) set the price expectation at zero.
- A per-student service may need a different budget and approver from a software licence.
- A whole cohort is far more than you can do by hand (section 7).

### 4.5 Scholarship bodies

**What they do.** They pay for the degree and decide which universities a scholar may attend
[Known]:

- Yayasan Khazanah (the foundation of Khazanah Nasional, the government's investment fund):
  universities "not on the list will not be considered".
- Bank Negara (Malaysia's central bank): approved pre-U colleges, then approved universities.
- JPA (Public Service Department, the main government scholarship body), top programme: "a
  choice of 20 universities from the QS World University Rankings".
- Yayasan Sime Darby (a company foundation): 10 named Malaysian universities.
- MARA (Majlis Amanah Rakyat, a government agency that sponsors Bumiputera students) "will guide
  students in choosing universities".

**Key numbers** [Known]:

- JPA spent RM38,115–277,515 per overseas scholar per year (2016 parliamentary reply, an
  official answer given in Parliament).
- MARA saves "between RM200,000 and RM300,000 in sponsorship costs per student" through
  Australian discounts of up to 30%.
- Yayasan Khazanah made 115 awards in 2024.
- MARA moved about 200 US-bound scholars to other countries in 2025 at short notice.

**Fit.** Strong on cost, weak on "any university".

- Total degree cost in MYR, and Reach/Match/Safety *within their approved list*, match how they
  already manage budgets [Guess].
- No sponsor was found paying anyone to shortlist today. The only example is a 2015 MARA cohort
  placed by IIE (Institute of International Education, a US non-profit) [snippet only].
- MARA buys through quotations limited to Bumiputera suppliers registered with the Ministry of
  Finance (section 6).

This is a later conversation, not a first one.

### 4.6 Private universities and branch campuses

**What they do.** Recruit students, and their job ads read like sales jobs [Known]:

- INTI: "Engage & influence prospects (potential students) using selling skills", with an "attractive sales incentive".
- UNITAR's counsellor role is titled "(Global Sales)".
- Heriot-Watt runs scholarships "to drive urgency and improve conversion rates".

**What winning a student costs them** [Known unless tagged]:

- Agent commission (RM1,000–2,000 flat where published).
- Marketing: SEGi spent RM9.33m in 2025, about 4.5% of revenue (total income).
- Bursaries used as discounts. Taylor's brochure says "The bursary is a form of discount".
- SEGi's accounts show **RM20.1m** of agent commission not yet counted as a cost at the end of
  2025, up from RM13.1m. SEGi spreads this cost over each student's years of study, so the figure
  is a running balance, *not* one year's spending. Don't quote it as "RM20m a year on agents".
- Taylor's converted 2.7% of online leads into enrolments [Known, but reported by SleekFlow, the
  software company selling the fix].

**Fit.** The most conflicted buyer. Their counsellors are measured on enrolling students into
*their own* programmes, and they pay to keep their own pre-U students (Taylor's up to RM8,000)
[Known]. The weaker-conflict corners, all Guess:

- Transfer programmes (see 4.3).
- Branch campuses, whose own sales pitch is total cost against the home campus.
- Research on rival universities for their own staff, which UCSI and UNITAR staff do by hand.
  That would be a different product, never shown to families.

## 6. How buying works, and the rules

### 6.1 Who decides

| Buyer type | Who decides | How it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Small agents, consultancies | Owner or founder | One meeting can be enough [Guess] |
| IDP and large agents | Regional or head office | Slow and central [Guess] |
| International schools | Counsellor proposes; head and bursar (the school's finance manager) approve | A yearly subscription, set when the budget is set [Guess, from how Cialfo and Unifrog are sold] |

### 6.3 PDPA: what changes when you handle a client's student data

In a B2B deal the school or firm is the **data controller** (decides why student data is used)
and you are its **data processor** (handle the data on its instructions). The 2024 amendment to
the law (Act A1727) and the 2025 guidelines from the PDP Commissioner (the regulator) put duties
on processors directly [Known; the Act and both guidelines were read in full]:

| Duty | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Keep the data secure** (in force 1 Apr 2025) | Take "practical steps" to protect it. Penalty up to RM1,000,000 or 3 years in prison |
| **Report breaches (leaks or unauthorised access) to the client "promptly"** (1 Jun 2025) | Their 72-hour clock to tell the regulator starts when you tell them, so expect this written into your contract |
| **Data Protection Officer** (1 Jun 2025) | A named person responsible for data protection. Only needed above 20,000 people, or 10,000 for sensitive or financial data. You are far below this [Guess] |
| **Data leaving Malaysia** (1 Apr 2025) | The client must be satisfied that data sent abroad is protected. Your pipeline sends student profiles through overseas services (Claude, Firecrawl), so each client has to accept that [Guess on application] |
| **Don't reuse their data** (since 2013) | A processor handles data "solely on behalf of" the controller. Reusing a client's student data for your own product would make you a controller, with the full set of duties. University research itself isn't personal data, so reusing *that* is fine [Known definition; Guess on application] |
| **Under-18s** (since 2013) | For anyone under 18, consent runs through the parent or guardian [Known] |

Colleges, universities and private schools are already registered data controllers, so expect them
to send you a data processing agreement (a contract setting out these duties) [Guess]. Keep any
family-budget field optional or in broad ranges rather than exact figures, since it may count as
financial information [Guess].

## 9. Interview script (Mom Test style)

The rule from *The Mom Test* (a book about customer interviews): ask about what people **did** and
**spent** in the past, never "would you buy this?". People are polite about the future and
truthful about the past.

**Opening (30 seconds).** "I'm a student building a university-shortlist service. I'm not selling
anything today. I'm trying to understand how [schools / consultancies / colleges] build a
student's university list now. Can I ask about the last few you did?"

**Core questions, for everyone**

1. Walk me through the last student whose university list you built. Who built it, how long did
   it take, and what did you open?
2. Where did the fee and entry-requirement figures come from? Who checked them, and when?
3. The last time a family asked what the whole degree would cost in ringgit, what did you give
   them, and how long did it take?
4. How many students does each person carry at the busiest point? What gets squeezed?
5. In the last year, did a list go wrong (wrong fee, missed requirement, course not recognised at
   home)? What did it cost you?
6. What did you spend last year on tools, data or outside help for this? Who signed it off, and
   when is it up for renewal?
7. The last time you started paying a new outside service, who suggested it, who signed, and how
   long from first meeting to payment?

**Add-ons by type** (pick two or three)

- *International-school counsellors:* Which platform do you use (Unifrog, Cialfo, BridgeU, a
  spreadsheet), and what does the school pay for it? Which platform suggestions made the final
  list, and which did you add by hand? Have agents or portals offered you free tools, and what did
  you say?
- *Paid consultants:* How do you price: per school, per package or per hour? Which part do families
  argue about? Do any universities you recommend pay you anything? (Ask Inquota this first.) What
  share of students go somewhere other than the US or UK, and who builds those lists?
- *Pre-U placement offices:* Which universities pay the college when a student enrols, and roughly
  what share of last year's placements went to them? Have you recommended a university with no
  agreement with you, and what happened?
- *Scholarship bodies:* How was this year's approved list built? When the US-bound scholars were
  moved in 2025, who did the re-placing and how long did it take? Have you ever paid an outside
  organisation to place scholars?
- *Agents and portals:* When a student asked about a university you don't partner with, what
  happened? How do new consultants learn the courses, and how long until they work alone? Has a
  partner university ever checked your advice against the UK or Australian code, and what did it
  ask to see?
- *Data (anyone you might pilot with):* Since the 2025 PDPA changes, has a vendor had to sign a
  data processing agreement with you? What did you insist on?

**Show the sample last, and only one made-up student.** After the past-behaviour questions, show a
sample list for a made-up student (never a real one). Ask what they would change, then: "If a list
like this put another university above one you'd normally recommend, what would happen?" Watch
their reaction; it answers the conflict question better than any direct question.

**What counts as a commitment** (from weakest to strongest):

| Signal | Counts? |
|---|---|
| "Interesting", "send me some info", "we'd definitely use this" | **No.** These are compliments |
| A second meeting with a date in it | A little: they give time |
| An introduction to the person who signs, or to a peer at another school or firm | Yes: they risk their reputation |
| Real student details with names removed, for a trial, under a data agreement | Yes: they give data and effort |
| A paid pilot with a named price, a student count and a start date | **Yes. This is the test** |

**After each call**, write down within the hour:

- what they did last time
- what they spent
- who signs
- the strongest commitment offered
- any surprise

These notes are the evidence this desk research cannot provide.
