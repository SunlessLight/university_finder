# Private universities and branch campuses: the recruitment side

*Stream 4. Researched 2026-10-15.*

## 1. Summary in 5 lines

1. Malaysia's private universities and foreign **branch campuses** (a foreign university's own campus in Malaysia, e.g. Monash, Nottingham, Heriot-Watt) run recruitment teams whose job ads read like sales jobs. Staff are measured on converting **leads** (enquiries from interested students) into enrolments, logging everything in a **CRM** (customer-tracking software) and hitting targets. Some ads promise a "sales incentive".
2. Getting a student costs them money in four ways. They pay agents per student (RM1,000–2,000 flat at the institutions that publish a rate). They spend on marketing (SEGi: RM9.3m in 2025). They give fee discounts presented as scholarships and bursaries. And they buy software. SEGi's 2025 accounts are the clearest public number: RM20.1m of agent commission paid but not yet expensed. The figure is up from RM13.1m a year earlier.
3. The market is crowded. There were 384 private institutions in 2024, down from 452 in 2018. About 527,000 Malaysians study at private institutions [derived from MOHE, the Ministry of Higher Education]. Taylor's reported that only 2.7% of its online leads became enrolments.
4. An honest any-university list **conflicts head-on** with their main goal, which is to keep their own pre-university students and enrol new ones. Taylor's, for example, offers up to RM8,000 off for staying on. Two places where the conflict is weaker: (a) **transfer programmes**, where the student leaves anyway and the institution helps pick the overseas university; (b) branch campuses, whose selling point is total cost compared with the home campus.
5. They are proven software buyers (Salesforce, SleekFlow, Agentforce). They are not proven buyers of *honest outside advice*. Whether any of them would put their name on a list that can rank a rival first is untested. That is the interview question.

## 3. Customers

- **Who they serve:** Malaysian school-leavers (SPM, the Malaysian O-Level equivalent, plus STPM, UEC and IGCSE) and their parents, their own pre-u and diploma students moving on to a degree, and international students.
- **Who pays them:** families, through tuition, often funded by PTPTN (the national student loan). Some students are sponsored. International students pay full fees. **Agents are paid by the institution**, not the student. MEDIU's contract and UTHM's scheme both forbid agents from charging students [Known, MEDIU agreement; UTHM page].
- **The internal customer:** the institution's own pre-u students. Taylor's and Sunway both pay bursaries only to students who *progress* (continue) into their own degree (section 4).

## 4. How they make money, and what a student costs them

Tuition is their income. Below is what they **spend to win a student**.

**Agent commission** (paid by the institution to the agent):

| Institution | Rate | Source and status |
|---|---|---|
| MEDIU (private) | RM300 short course, RM1,000 pre-u/diploma, RM2,000 undergraduate, RM1,500 postgraduate. Paid RM1,500 + RM500 over two semesters. Nothing paid for students who already have a record there | [Known, MEDIU agreement PDF; file dated 2016, so possibly out of date] |
| USIM (public) | RM1,500 per student, paid as RM750 per semester. The second payment is withheld if the student drops out | [Known, usim.edu.my, 2026 windows] |
| UTHM (public) | RM2,000 per student to an agent, RM1,000 to an "introducer". Agents pay a RM500 registration fee | [Known, uthm.edu.my, updated Aug 2026] |
| Uni Enrol (portal, paid by private institutions) | 10% of first-year tuition | Stream 1's finding (Vulcan Post 2020, sponsored article) |
| Large private groups and branch campuses (Taylor's, Sunway, Monash and the rest) | **Not published** | [Unknown: searched agent pages, MAPCU (the private colleges and universities association), NAPEI (the private educational institutions association), ICEF and PIE News. No Malaysian percentage found] |

- **SEGi's accounts confirm commission is material.** "The Group pays agent commission to its agent for certain contracts that they obtain for number of students recruited... sales commissions are amortised over two to three years." *Amortised* means the cost is spread over the student's study years rather than charged at once. The unexpensed balance (**contract cost asset**) was **RM20.1m at end-2025, up from RM13.1m** [Known, SEGi AR2025, notes 2.22 and 20].
  - That balance is about 10% of a year's revenue [estimate, RM20.1m ÷ RM207.5m]. It is a balance, not a year's spending, so don't quote it as "commission paid".
  - SEGi's Q1 2024 profit fell 22% "due to higher acquisition cost for students at the time of enrolment" [Known, The Edge, May 2024].
- **Overseas agents now recruit into Malaysia at scale.** IDP (the biggest listed agent) announced Malaysia as a new destination in April 2026, in a "phased pilot" [Known, PIE News]. EMGS ran an agent workshop that brought together 70+ agencies and 30+ Malaysian institutions [Known from snippet only, EMGS 2022 release]. A 2012 survey put **56%** of international students at Malaysian institutions as agent-recruited [Known from snippet only, Inside Higher Ed citing OBHE, the Observatory on Borderless Higher Education; old].

**Marketing spend:**
- SEGi spent **RM9.33m in 2025 (RM8.01m in 2024)**, about 4.5% of revenue [Known for the figures, SEGi AR2025 income statement; the % is my arithmetic]. Commission sits elsewhere in the accounts, so this understates the total cost of winning students [Guess, from the separate contract-cost note].
- Penang Institute says institutions commonly spend "10% to 20% or more of revenue on marketing" but admits there is no clear Malaysian data [Known, Penang Institute].

**Scholarships and bursaries used as discounts:**
- Taylor's 2026 brochure says: "The bursary is a form of discount... advised by the appointed Counsellors at the point of enrolment" [Known].
- Taylor's TEG Bursary for students who progress from Taylor's College pre-u or foundation into a Taylor's degree within a year is **up to RM8,000 off tuition**. Advanced Diploma progression is up to RM4,000 [Known, brochure p.23].
- Sunway College Johor Bahru's **Continuing Bursary** gives **10% off tuition** for the whole degree to pre-u and diploma students who progress within 12 months [Known, 2026 PDF].
- Heriot-Watt Malaysia's recruiter ad lists this duty: "Support the implementation of scholarships, bursaries, and promotional campaigns to drive urgency and improve conversion rates" [Known, JobStreet]. This is an employer saying in writing that scholarships are a sales tool.
- Scale: the Jeffrey Cheah Foundation awarded RM75m in scholarships and grants at its 2025 ceremony, RM967m+ in total [Known, Sunway University news, Nov 2025]. SEGi's merit scholarships are small: RM173k plus RM759k in 2025 [Known, SEGi AR2025].

**Software spend:**
- Taylor's uses Salesforce plus SleekFlow, a WhatsApp/chat inbox [Known, SleekFlow case study].
- UNM deployed Salesforce's "Agentforce" AI chatbot (NOVA) to answer questions on "courses, eligibility fees, and scholarships" [Known, UNM press release Dec 2025].
- SEGi rolled out its own campus system, SPARK, which includes "Admission and Enrolment" [Known, AR2025].

**Cost per enrolment:** no institution publishes it [Unknown: searched annual reports, press releases and vendor case studies]. One proxy: at Taylor's 2.7% online lead-to-enrolment rate, about **37 online leads produce one enrolment** [estimate, 1 ÷ 0.027].

## 5. Pain points

All evidence-backed:

1. **Conversion targets and pipeline pressure.**
   - Heriot-Watt Malaysia wants "a results-driven individual with strong conversion capabilities" to "manage both direct and agent-driven pipelines" [Known].
   - INTI's school-partnerships ad says: "Ensure that every engagement activity results in captured and usable leads, not just attendance" and "Be accountable for pipeline movement, not just activity volume". It tracks every student through "Lead → Appointment → Invited walk-in / Direct walk-in → Application → Enrolment" [Known].
   - UCSI's agent-management ad sets "stretched targets at minimum growth rate of 20% per year" [Known, deadline Aug 2024].
2. **Counsellors are salespeople.**
   - INTI's "Education Counselor, International Office" must "Engage & influence prospects using selling skills & INTI Program Counselling Methodologies" and "nurture and harvest applications to meet aligned sales target", with an "Attractive sales incentive" [Known].
   - UNITAR's counsellor role is titled "(Global Sales)", and its KPIs (key performance indicators, the numbers staff are judged on) include "Lead-to-application and application-to-enrollment conversion" [Known].
   - JobStreet files several of these counsellor roles under the **Sales** category [Known, listing pages].
3. **Low conversion and slow response.** Taylor's: online leads converted at **2.7%**. First response time was **1 hour 20 minutes** before new software. Staff had to "manually" copy chat data into Salesforce [Known, SleekFlow, vendor-reported].
4. **Out-of-hours enquiries and admin load.** UNM: international enquiries "often arrive outside local office hours", and admin work takes time away from talking to students [Known, UNM release].
5. **Weekends, travel, school visits.** Ads ask for "alternate weekends and Public Holidays" (INTI) and "weekends and travel (domestic and international)" (Heriot-Watt). UCSI wants "Willingness to travel frequently within Malaysia" [Known].
6. **Competitor research done by hand.** UCSI: "conduct market survey and intelligence amongst main competitors in private education industry". UNITAR: "Monitor competitor activities, market trends and student demand" [Known].
7. **Sector squeeze.**
   - Institution count fell from 452 to 384 between 2018 and 2024. Penang Institute cites "excessive market competition straining financial resources" and "intensified price competition" amid a falling birth rate [Known].
   - SEGi's 2024 profit fell on student acquisition cost [Known].
8. **Bad-agent risk on the international side.** A USM (Universiti Sains Malaysia) academic writes that an "obscure private college desperate to fill seats will pay generously and ask few questions". He says MOHE is "shutting down colleges and tightening scrutiny of Bangladeshi applications" [Known, Prothom Alo op-ed, Sep 2026; opinion].

## 6. How they shortlist today

On their side, "shortlisting" means **picking one of *their own* programmes for the student**, not comparing universities.

- **Programme counselling.** Heriot-Watt counsellors give "personalised counselling to prospective students and their parents". IMU's "Senior Programme Advisor" guides students "in selecting courses and programs at IMU University" [Known]. **The list contains one institution** [Guess, from the job wording].
- **Free assessments used as lead magnets** (free offers that collect a student's contact details). UCSI open days advertised a "Free Career Matching Analysis" [Known, ucsiuniversity.edu.my, 2019 event]. So "help you choose" is already used to bring families in the door [Guess].
- **Transfer and 2+1 programmes** (part of the degree in Malaysia, the rest overseas):
  - This is the one place the institution *does* build a list of other universities. Sunway's American degree programme runs a "dedicated transfer support, and placement office" with workshops on "selecting suitable universities" and helping students into "'best fit' colleges" [Known, Sunway School of American Education].
  - Sunway also calls Arizona State University (ASU) its "exclusive partner", with "over 20 pathways to ASU" [Known]. So even this list has a preferred destination built in.
- **Pre-u students who might leave.** The retention offer is a bursary given by the "appointed Counsellors at the point of enrolment" (Taylor's) [Known]. Pre-u college counselling offices are stream 3's subject. The point for this stream is that **the same group earns more if the counsellor's student stays** [Guess, from the progression-only bursaries].
- **Total cost in MYR:** no institution I checked gives a whole-degree ringgit comparison against other universities [Unknown: checked Taylor's, Sunway, UCSI, Nottingham Malaysia fee and scholarship pages]. Fee pages list only their own fees.
- **Time per student and tools:** CRM (Salesforce at Taylor's and UNM), chat inboxes and campus systems [Known]. Minutes spent per counselling session: [Unknown].

## 7. Fit with an honest, family-first list

**The core conflict** [Guess, from sections 4–6]: the institution pays agents and offers discounts to win *this* student. A list that can rank a rival first, show the rival's lower total cost, or give "reasons not to go" to the institution itself works against its targets. Its counsellors are measured on conversion to *their* programmes. An institution that buys the list and hands it to a family is paying to risk losing that student. Expect requests to drop "reasons not to go" or to filter to their own programmes. **That breaks the product's promise.** Decide in advance whether you would accept it. Stream 1 reached the same conclusion for agents.

**Where the conflict is weaker** (all [Guess], to test in interviews):

| Use | Why it might fit | Where it still bites |
|---|---|---|
| **Transfer, 2+1 and American degree programme placement offices** | The student is leaving anyway. The institution has already earned the Malaysian years' fees. Sunway already runs workshops on "selecting suitable universities". A total-cost-in-MYR comparison of overseas destinations is real work they do now | Preferred partners (ASU at Sunway) and credit-transfer agreements. A list that ranks a non-partner first may still be unwelcome |
| **Branch campuses competing on cost against the home campus** | The total-cost-in-MYR column is their own sales pitch. Nottingham itself once pitched its Malaysia campus as roughly £30,000 total versus £46,000 for England [Known from snippet only, THE, early 2010s; old figures] | Only helps while they are cheapest. Once "a better option" shows up on the list, the conflict returns |
| **Internal market-intelligence data** (never shown to families) | UCSI and UNITAR ads already ask staff to research competitors by hand. An any-university dataset with fees and entry fit is that research | It is a different product (competitor data, not a family list). It also does nothing for the family-first mission |
| **Free list as a lead magnet** | Institutions already give away "career matching" to attract families | The worst conflict. A list branded by the institution that steers to itself is just marketing. An honest one gives away leads |

**Revenue logic, if any** [Guess]: the institution would only pay if the list helps it convert or retain. A 2.7% conversion rate (Taylor's online leads) and agent commissions of RM1,000–2,000 (published rates) set the scale of what an extra enrolment is worth. This is not evidence of willingness to pay. Treat it as a ceiling-setting question for interviews.

## 9. Questions to ask them

Ask about past behaviour and real spending. Never ask "would you buy this?"

1. Think of your last intake. How many enquiries did you get, how many enrolled, and which channel converted best? (Ask for the actual numbers, not the target.)
2. What did one enrolled student cost you last year, all in? That means commission, marketing and the bursary. Which part is biggest?
3. What commission do you pay a Malaysian agent per degree student, and what do you pay an overseas agent? Has it gone up in the last two years?
4. The last time one of your pre-u students said they were going overseas instead, what did the counsellor do? What did you offer?
5. (Transfer or 2+1 offices) Walk me through how the last student's list of overseas universities was built. Who built it, how long did it take, and did it include universities outside your partner list?
6. Has a family ever asked your counsellor what the whole degree costs compared with another university? What did they get?
7. Your job ads mention competitor research. Who did it last time, what did they look at, and how long did it take?
8. The last time you bought a recruitment tool (CRM, chat or lead-generation service), who signed off, what did it cost, and how did you judge whether it worked?
9. Have you ever paid for outside advice or data that was *not* about your own institution? What was it?
10. If a list we gave a student ranked another university above you, what would your team do with it? (A reaction to something real, not a hypothetical "would you buy?" question: show them a sample.)
