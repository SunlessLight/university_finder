# Pre-university colleges, international schools and scholarship bodies

*Stream 3. Researched 2026-10-15.*

## 1. Summary in 5 lines

1. Three different buyers sit in this stream. **Pre-university (pre-U) colleges** (Taylor's, Sunway, HELP, INTI, Methodist College KL) run in-house "university placement" offices. **International schools** (Alice Smith, ISKL, KTJ, Garden, IGBIS and others) employ university counsellors. **Scholarship bodies** (MARA, JPA, Yayasan Khazanah, Bank Negara, Petronas, Yayasan Sime Darby) pay for the degree and limit which universities a scholar may attend.
2. For the student, all three are free. Who pays the counsellor differs. At least two pre-U colleges also sit on universities' **paid-agent lists**: Taylor's Malaysia Sdn Bhd is on the University of Liverpool's approved-agent list, and INTI appears on QUT's "other agents" list. So a college placement office can earn commission (a fee the university pays per enrolled student) like an agent does. International-school counsellors are paid from school fees. Scholarship bodies are paid by the government or a company.
3. **Scholarship bodies, not students, set the university list.** Khazanah, Bank Negara, Yayasan Sime Darby and JPA's top programme all say "approved universities only". MARA calls itself the body that "will guide students in choosing universities". Sponsors spend RM38,115 to RM277,515 per overseas scholar each year (JPA, 2016 parliamentary reply). MARA now bargains with universities directly for 20–30% tuition discounts. I found no evidence that any sponsor pays a placement agent today. The only example is a 2015 MARA cohort placed by IIE (search snippet only).
4. **Caseload numbers are thin.** KTJ's sixth form has 300 students, and it is hiring a single "designated US counsellor". ISKL lists a seven-person high-school counselling team, and that number includes an educational psychologist. Government schools work at 1 counsellor to 500 students, and those posts are mostly for mental health. No pre-U college publishes how many placement advisors it has.
5. Pain-point evidence is mostly indirect: placement KPIs (targets staff are measured on) in Taylor's job ads, school counsellor jobs that mix student wellbeing with university applications, and sponsors that keep changing their country lists (MARA moved about 200 US-bound scholars in 2025). I found no forum or review evidence that counsellors are short of total-cost data. The direct "pain" question has to be asked in interviews.

## 4. How they make money

- **Pre-U colleges** earn tuition from pre-U students. Placement is a selling point that gets students to enrol: "Experienced counsellors from our university placement centre further support you" (Sunway A-Level guide). Taylor's and Sunway publish Oxbridge and UCL/LSE outcomes as marketing [Known, Taylor's news page snippet; Sunway news archive snippet]. A second income source, commission on outward placements, is plausible but unproven (see section 3) [Guess].
- **International schools** charge fees. One report quotes RM50,000–70,000 a year for secondary, but that is a parent's estimate, not data [Known as quoted, Asia News Network]. Since 1 July 2025, a 6% service tax applies to fees above RM60,000 per student per year for non-citizens [snippet only, third-party guide]. I found no counselling budget line for any school [Unknown, looked at school pages, AIMS and job ads].
- **Scholarship bodies** spend, they don't earn. Known spend figures:
  - JPA: RM38,115–277,515 per overseas scholar per year, varying by field and country. In 2016 there were 7,736 government-sponsored students abroad, and cutting 744 places saved "up to RM240 million" a year [Known, FMT 17 Mar 2016, written parliamentary reply]. That works out to about RM322,600 per cut place per year [estimate, my arithmetic].
  - MARA: Saves "between RM200,000 and RM300,000 in sponsorship costs per student" through Australian discounts of up to 30% [Known, NST Feb 2026]. That implies a full sponsorship of roughly RM0.7–1m per overseas scholar for the whole degree [estimate, from 30% ≈ RM200–300k]. Al-Azhar medicine costs RM350,000 per student after a 50% discount [Known, Bernama 2026].
  - Yayasan Khazanah: RM20 million for 72 new scholars in 2013, about RM278,000 each [estimate from a Known figure, Bernama Aug 2013]. It had made 1,610 awards in total by Sep 2024, including 115 that year [Known, yayasanhasanah.org press release].
  - Johor state: RM5 million for its overseas SPM scheme, and a state official said UK study could cost over RM1 million per person [snippet only, Malay Mail 2024, which returned 403].

## 5. Pain points

All the evidence here is indirect. No counsellor I could find has said "we lack total-cost data" in public.

1. **Placement staff are measured on outcomes.** Snippets of Taylor's College job ads say the advisor is "responsible for achieving the desired outcomes and KPIs related to placing all students to the universities of their choice". Advisors are "country custodians", for example one for UK institutions and UK branch campuses in Malaysia and another for Australia. They track visa documents such as the CAS, COE, ATAS and I-20 (the confirmation letters UK, Australian and US visas require) [snippet only. I opened three Taylor's and Hiredly ads and all were expired with no text]. Having one advisor per country suggests nobody's job is to compare countries side by side on cost [Guess].
2. **One counsellor, many jobs.** KTJ's single US counsellor must also be a sixth-form tutor, teach PSHEE (personal, social and health education), write references, run fairs and keep destination records [Known, KTJ JD]. At IGBIS, wellbeing counselling and college counselling are one role [Known, IGBIS JD]. Pay at the lower end is low: RM3,000–4,000 a month for a school counsellor at Regent International School, and RM2,900–4,000 at Han Chiang (Penang) [Known, JobStreet listings via Firecrawl].
3. **Caseload pressure is a public issue in government schools.** The ratio is 1 counsellor to 500 secondary students, and a child psychiatrist called it "outdated" in Aug 2026. The Ministry's 1:250 target has not been reached [Known, NST 17 Aug 2026]. Those counsellors focus on mental health, not university choice. That gap is where agents and portals step in [Guess].
4. **Sponsors' country lists keep moving.** MARA stopped sending about 200 scholars to the US in 2025 and had to place them elsewhere at short notice [Known, Sinar Harian]. MARA is also looking at 2+2 or 3+1 "split" degrees (part of the degree in Malaysia, the rest abroad) in Australia to save cost [Known, NST]. Each change means re-shortlisting a whole cohort against a new list and budget [Guess].
5. **Sponsors care about cost per scholar.** MARA negotiates discounts per university and states the saving in RM per student [Known]. An honest list that shows the total degree cost in MYR speaks the same language as a sponsor's budget [Guess].
6. **Records and data.** KTJ wants "thorough and accurate records of... outcomes and final destinations". IGBIS wants "data-based" counsel [Known]. Both point to a reporting need, but neither names a tool.

## 6. How they shortlist today

- **Pre-U colleges:** A placement advisor (Taylor's, MCKL), a central office (Sunway IOUP, HELP SPC, INTI UPO) and lecturer-mentors who write references (Sunway). Inputs are partner lists, credit-transfer tables (INTI: 150+), resource libraries with "tuition fees, cost of living" (INTI) and university fairs (Taylor's runs an annual on-campus fair and the HELP SPC runs fairs "regularly") [Known]. Tools named in ads: Microsoft Office and Canva (MCKL) [Known]. How long a shortlist takes: [Unknown, not found in any source].
- **International schools:** One-to-one advising from Year 10 or 11, plus a platform where the school has one. Alice Smith uses Unifrog [Known]. Cempaka uses Uni Enrol's Pathway Match [Known]. KTJ runs Common App and UCAS (the UK central application service) without naming a list-building tool [Known]. Cialfo and BridgeU run counsellor workshops in Southeast Asia, but I could not confirm any named Malaysian school using them [Unknown, two searches. This belongs to stream 5].
- **Scholarship bodies:** The sponsor publishes an approved list, usually built from QS rankings by field (JPA PPN uses "20 universities from the QS World University Rankings". MARA's 2020 postgraduate guideline lists JPA- and professional-body-approved universities in an appendix) [Known]. Inside that list, the scholar's college counsellor (KYUEM, KTJ, Taylor's, INTI or a MARA college) helps choose and apply [Guess, from where scholars study].

## 7. Fit with an honest, family-first list

- **Pre-U colleges: mixed.** The fit is good because placement results are their marketing, and HELP and INTI already hand out cost estimates [Known]. The conflict: Taylor's, Sunway, HELP and INTI all own universities that compete for the same students. Sunway's placement brochure lists Sunway University among destinations, and Taylor's UPS also serves "Taylor's University Transfer students" [Known]. At least Taylor's and INTI also appear on paid-agent lists [Known/snippet]. A list that ranks a non-paying overseas university above the in-house university or a partner could cut into income [Guess]. It would probably be easier to sell as a back-office research tool for the advisor than as something handed to families under the college's name [Guess, the same logic as stream 1].
- **International schools: the best fit on paper.** Counsellors are paid by school fees, not by universities [Known for fee-funded schools, Guess that no university money reaches them]. IGBIS asks for "impartial" counsel [Known]. Families paying RM50k+ a year already expect overseas options [Guess]. The catch: the free, university-funded tools (Uni Enrol, and possibly BridgeU, see stream 5) set the price at zero.
- **Scholarship bodies: fit on cost, not on "any university".** Sponsors limit choice to approved lists, so "covers any university" matters less to them. Total cost in MYR and Reach/Match/Safety *within the list* fit directly with how they budget (cost per scholar, discount per student) [Guess]. A sponsor hiring an outside firm to shortlist would be a procurement (government purchasing) decision, and I found no sign that one exists today [Unknown, MARA and JPA searches in English and Malay].

## 9. Questions to ask them

*Pre-U placement offices*
1. Walk me through the last student you helped pick universities. Where did the first list come from, and how long did it take?
2. How many students does each advisor carry in peak season (Oct–Jan)? How many advisors were there last year?
3. When a student asks "what will this cost in total", what do you hand them today? Where does that number come from?
4. Which universities pay the college when a student enrols, and roughly what share of last year's placements went to them?
5. In the past year, have you recommended a university that has no agreement with you? What happened?
6. What did you spend last year on software, fairs or subscriptions for university research?

*International-school counsellors*
1. How many Year 13 / Grade 12 students did you personally carry last cycle, and what else is on your plate?
2. Which platform do you use today (Unifrog, Cialfo, BridgeU, Maia, a spreadsheet)? Who signed off the cost, and when is it up for renewal?
3. Tell me about the last parent who pushed back on a list. What did they want that you didn't have?
4. When did you last have to estimate a total degree cost in ringgit? How did you do it?
5. Have agents or portals approached you to offer free tools? What did you say?

*Scholarship bodies*
1. How was this year's approved-university list built, and by whom (staff, a consultant, rankings)?
2. When MARA moved the US-bound scholars in 2025, how long did re-placing them take and who did the work?
3. Do you track total cost per scholar by university before approving a placement? Where does that sit today?
4. Have you ever paid an outside organisation (IIE, an agent, a college) to place scholars? What did it cost?
5. How many scholars end up at a university other than their first choice, and why?
