## 1. Summary in 5 lines

1. "Paid consultant" covers three very different businesses in Malaysia: **(a)** a global premium firm, Crimson Education, sold to Malaysian families from its Singapore regional team; **(b)** small local or foreign boutiques aimed at international-school students applying to US/UK elite universities (PrepWorks, Axiom Learning, TigerCampus, Ivy Summit, InGenius Prep, True North); **(c)** "hybrid" study-abroad firms like Inquota that charge families a fee *and* list partner universities, so they sit halfway to the agents in stream 1.
2. Prices span about **RM2,000** (Inquota's entry package) to **RM100,000+** (Crimson "upwards of USD 25,000"; Ivy Summit premium packages USD 20,000–50,000). Almost no Malaysian firm publishes a price. Crimson, PrepWorks, Axiom, TigerCampus and InGenius all say "free consultation, then a quote".
3. The university list is built by **one senior person** (Crimson calls them a "Strategist") as one task in a multi-year package that is mostly essays, activities and test prep. Crimson's job ad says the strategist creates "lists of best fit schools of varied competition" (a Reach/Match/Safety spread in plain words). Former Crimson strategists on Glassdoor report **40–50 students each** and call it "an assembly line".
4. The family pays them, so there is **no commission conflict** like the agents have. But the marketing scoreboard is "Ivy League and Oxbridge offers", and none of the sites I opened shows total degree cost in ringgit or reasons *not* to go somewhere.
5. The strongest pain-point evidence is about **staff overload and price/contract pressure**, not about list quality. I found **no Malaysian forum or review evidence**. Lowyat and Reddit could not be reached or returned nothing. This is the biggest gap.

## 3. Customers

- **Who they serve:** students aiming at selective US/UK universities, mostly from KL international schools (Alice Smith, ISKL and GIS are named by both Crimson and Ivy Summit) and A-Level/IB programmes [Known]. Crimson and Ivy Summit start as young as age 11 ("Crimson Rise", ages 10–14) [Known, Crimson job ad and Vulcan Post]. Inquota's customer is different: an ordinary study-abroad family going to Australia, the UK or Canada. It asks for SPM/STPM/diploma transcripts, IELTS and visa history [Known, inquota.com.my].
- **Who pays them:** the **family**, directly. No firm in types A or B mentions university commission [Known that they don't mention it; whether none takes any is Unknown]. Inquota is the exception to check (see above).
- **Who they target:** a leaked 2019 Crimson sales playbook (New Zealand) told reps to score families on "willingness, affluence, candidacy" and **not to pursue** families unlikely to pay over NZ$15,000. Families able to pay NZ$30,000+ were the "primary target" [Known, The Spinoff, 14 Nov 2019; old and from New Zealand, so not proof of current Malaysian practice].

## 5. Pain points

All evidence below is from sources I opened unless marked. **None of it is Malaysian-specific.** That is a finding in itself: I found no Lowyat, Reddit or Malaysian review thread on paid consultants (Reddit blocked my fetch, and Lowyat returned nothing).

1. **Caseload overload for the person who builds the list.**
   - "High workload, 40+ student cases per strategist. Feels like an assembly line of students" [Known, Glassdoor, former Senior Strategy Consultant].
   - "Strategists work with 45-50 students" [Known, Glassdoor].
   - Glassdoor's own summary says "high workload and caseloads often lead to stress… burnout is a real concern during peak seasons" [Known].
   - The Asia strategist job ad asks for "supporting a high number of students through the application process" and "achieving the number of students as agreed" [Known].
   - "Taking time off just means catching up with more later on" [Known, Glassdoor].
2. **Pay squeeze and difficult parents.** "There are no systems in place to protect strategists from rude parents… strategists get paid $60-70k even when they come with YEARS of experience" [Known, Glassdoor]. The Strategy Consultant rating is 3.5/5 from 36 reviews [Known].
3. **Sales pressure and trust.**
   - Reps were briefed to contact leads "six to nine times in under a month" [Known, Spinoff 2019].
   - "Ghost offices" and untrained-tutor allegations were made in Dec 2019 (Crimson rejected them) [Known, Wikipedia].
   - Outcome claims are not comparable across firms: Crimson's offer counts differ between its own pages, Inquota's placements read both 200+ and 2000+, and Axiom and True North give claims with no numbers [Known]. A family has no neutral way to check them [Guess].
4. **Cost is not part of their product.** A competitor (Orbit, which sells a scholarship tool, so it is biased) argues that premium consultants answer "a narrower question, admissions strategy", and leave the cost and scholarship side "largely unaddressed" [Known, findmyorbit.com]. I found **no paid consultant page that mentions total degree cost** in any currency [Known for the pages listed in section 10].
5. **Stigma.** Crimson's Singapore lead said that on entering Singapore "there was a stigma around getting help with university admissions" [Known, Vulcan Post]. Whether this holds in Malaysia is [Unknown].
6. **The elite segment is small.** 22 Cambridge admits from Malaysia in 2024 [Known]. Boutiques compete for a few hundred Ivy/Oxbridge hopefuls a year [Guess, from those counts]. This may be why PrepWorks also sells tutoring and boarding-school help, and Axiom and TigerCampus are tutoring businesses first [Guess].
7. **Customer reviews of Crimson are mostly positive.** Trustpilot summarises 138 reviews as praising "personalized and attentive guidance" and the app/portal. One parent wrote "if you're searching and get surprised by the program fee, don't be" [Known, Trustpilot]. So the pain sits with staff and price, not obviously with satisfied clients.

## 6. How they shortlist today

- **Who builds the list:** one named senior adviser.
  - Crimson: the "Strategy Consultant… crafts your overarching university application plan; helps you build a school list and refine your application theme". The other team members are essay mentors, tutors, extracurricular mentors, a Student Success Manager (deadlines) and a Former Admissions Officer (reviews the application) [Known, Crimson Malaysia page].
  - Reviews mention a "country strategist" and a "global strategist" [Known, Trustpilot].
  - One Malaysia-page testimonial: the strategist "provided useful stats on acceptance rates" to pick a Cambridge college [Known].
- **The method is described only loosely:**
  - Crimson job ad: "create lists of best fit schools of varied competition" [Known].
  - Ivy Summit: learn preferences (size, social life, academic rigour), "balance" them with ambitions, to "maximize admission chances at dream schools" while finding a school the student will enjoy [Known].
  - Inquota: based on "academic results, budget, intended intake, course preference, English test status" and visa history [Known].
  - Axiom: "best-fit universities based on your academic profile, interests, and career goals" [Known].
  - PrepWorks: "career profiling, personalised university recommendations" [Known].
- **Tools:**
  - Crimson uses its own "Crimson App" for session notes and actions [Known, job ad]. It also publishes US and UK "admissions calculators" (chance estimators) [Known from search summary only].
  - Boutiques name no software [Known that none is named]. Stream 5 covers Cialfo/BridgeU, which school counsellors use. I saw no evidence that consultants use them [Unknown].
- **How long it takes:** no firm states it [Unknown]. List building is folded into multi-year packages. Crimson starts at age 11–14, Ivy Summit at grades 7–12, and Inquota quotes 6–8 months from first consultation to visa [Known].
- **Coverage:** Crimson, the boutiques and Ivy Summit focus on top US/UK (Ivy, Top 15/20/30, Oxbridge, Imperial, LSE, UCL) [Known]. Inquota covers wider destinations, including Australia, Canada, Germany and Italy [Known]. I saw no evidence that any of them builds lists that include Malaysian private universities or cheaper destinations [Unknown; not mentioned on any page opened].

## 7. Fit with an honest, family-first list

- **No commission conflict at types A and B.** The family pays, so an any-university list does not cut into their income the way it does for agents [Known that the family pays; Guess that this removes the conflict]. **Inquota (type C) may have the agent conflict** if it takes partner commission [Guess, to confirm in interview].
- **Where it could help them** [all Guess, to test in interviews]:
  - A strategist with 40–50 students has no time for research-heavy work (verifying fees, computing total cost in MYR, checking MQA recognition). That work is exactly what the founder's product does. It could be a back-office research input that the strategist reviews and presents under their own brand.
  - Lower-priced firms (Inquota at RM2,000, TigerCampus at hourly rates) cannot afford hours of research per student at their prices. Bought-in research could protect their margin.
  - Total cost in MYR and "reasons not to go" are a feature no consultant advertises (section 5, point 4).
- **Where it could hurt them** [Guess]:
  - The list is part of what justifies a RM30,000–100,000 package. A cheap, good list sold straight to families undercuts the "strategy" value. Consultants may buy it only white-labelled (sold under their name, not the founder's).
  - Their scoreboard is elite offers. An honest list that says "this Reach is not worth the cost" may clash with a business that markets Ivy/Oxbridge counts. On the other hand, a firm that is paid per package (not per admit) has no direct loss from that advice.
  - Elite-focused firms may see a Malaysia-plus-anywhere total-cost list as off-target for their US/UK-only clients.
- **The test to run:** ask whether list-building time is a bottleneck (section 9). If strategists spend little time on lists, the research product is not their pain.

## 9. Questions to ask them

Ask about what they did and spent in the past, not about whether they would buy (Mom Test style).

1. "Walk me through the last student whose university list you built. Who did it, and how many hours did it take?"
2. "Where did the fee, entry-requirement and cost figures on that list come from? Who checked them, and when?"
3. "How many students does each of your consultants carry right now? What gets squeezed in peak season (Oct–Jan)?"
4. "In the last year, did you lose or refund a family because of a list problem, such as a wrong fee, a missed requirement or a course that wasn't recognised back home?"
5. "Have you ever paid for outside research, data or software to help build lists? What was it, what did it cost, and why did you keep or drop it?"
6. "When a family asks 'how much will the whole degree cost in ringgit?', what do you give them today?"
7. "How do you price: per school, per package or per hour? Which part of the package do families argue about?"
8. "Do any of the universities you recommend pay you anything (commission, event fees, partnership)?" (Especially Inquota.)
9. "What share of your students end up somewhere other than the US/UK, for example Malaysian private universities, Australia or Europe? Who builds those lists?"
10. "When did you last hire a consultant? What did the job ad ask for, and how long did it take to fill?"
