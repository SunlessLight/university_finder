# Education agents and free portals in Malaysia

*Stream 1. Researched 2026-10-15.*

## 1. Summary in 5 lines

1. Malaysia has two kinds of "free" helper. **Study-abroad agents** (IDP, MABECS, AECC, SI-UK, AUG, JM Education, UKEC and many smaller firms) mostly send students to the UK and Australia. **Online portals** (Uni Enrol, EduAdvisor, EasyUni) mostly send students to Malaysian private colleges, and Uni Enrol and EduAdvisor now do study-abroad as well.
2. All of them are free to the family and paid by the institution. The usual payment is a **commission** (a fee the university pays the agent for each student who enrols). UK figures run about 10% to 20%+ of first-year tuition. Australian rates are reported to be up to twice the UK level. One Malaysian portal said it earns 10% of first-year tuition.
3. The one public set of accounts is IDP's (listed in Australia). In FY25 (the year to June 2025) IDP made A$367m from 70,076 placements, about A$5,237 per student. It does not report Malaysia on its own.
4. The front-line staff are "education consultants" on a basic salary of RM3,000 to RM6,000 plus commission tied to enrolment targets. Their job ads ask them to answer students' messages at night, work weekends and turn enquiries into enrolments.
5. Agents are only paid when a student goes to a partner (client) university, and IDP's own footnote says so. That is the core conflict with an any-university, honest list. It is also why such a list might work for agents as a **back-office research tool** but not as something given to families under the agent's brand. That is a guess, and it needs testing in interviews.

## 3. Customers

- **Who they serve:** students and their parents, usually at SPM or A-Level/foundation results time. Uni Enrol's job ad names "peak period (after exam results)" [Known].
- **Who pays them:** the institution, in almost every case.
  - Uni Enrol: "the university pays a service fee" [Known, unienrol.com].
  - MABECS: "Our fees are covered by partner universities as part of our recruitment agreements" [Known, mabecs.com].
  - EduAdvisor: "For every successful application we submit to a partner institution, EduAdvisor earns a small fee." It also lists "funds from investors, referral fees from colleges and revenues from advertisers" [Known, eduadvisor.my/help-centre].
  - EasyUni: "We make money from the services we offer to universities and colleges" [Known, easyuni.com/about].
  - IDP's annual results say placement revenue is "all commissions received from institutions… plus any fees paid by students" [Known, FY25 presentation]. So some students pay IDP for some services, but most of the revenue is commission.
- **Exceptions where the family pays:** SI-UK's paid "Premium Application Service" [Known from snippet], and AECC lists "Premium Counselling" on its menu with no price shown [Known, aeccglobal.com/my]. These suggest that some agents already sell paid add-ons to families [Guess].
- **A second customer: the university.** Portals sell universities "verified lead generation" (contacts of interested students), premium listings, advertising and events [Known for EasyUni from easyuni.com/about and the yespress profile, which I saw only as a snippet]. IDP earns "Other services" revenue from institutions for "digital marketing, events, consultancy and data services" [Known, FY25 presentation]. In other words, the university is often a bigger customer than the student.

## 4. How they make money

**Commission rates paid by UK universities** (UK contracts are confidential, so these are reported figures):

- In 2011/12 the average was about £1,000 per student, about 10% of fees. Nottingham paid a flat £1,450 [Known, PIE News 2013].
- In 2013-14 the average was £1,767 per student, and 106 institutions spent £86.7m in total [Known, THE headline via search].
- In 2021/22, PIE News worked out averages per student from Freedom of Information requests: Exeter £3,529, Kingston £3,055, Durham £2,819, Kent £1,659. It also reported fears that rates are rising above the "standard 10%", with "large agents… 20% or more" [Known, PIE News, 5 Aug 2022].
- 10% to 30% of first-year fees is a reported range (Exeposé citing a 2023 Big Issue investigation). The same source says UK universities spend about £500m a year on agents and that agents handle about 50% of international admissions [Known, but from one source, second-hand].
- **Typical contract terms.** UEA claws commission back if the student withdraws in the first semester, pays nothing for students who start after the second academic year, and the university may divide commission between two agents at its own discretion [Known, UEA FOI 25-270]. Edinburgh says from the September 2026 intake it will pay a **partial commission** to agents who counsel a student after the student has applied. It does not split 50/50, and it pays nothing "if there is evidence… the Representative has not acted in the best interest of the student" [Known, ed.ac.uk agent guidance].
- **Sub-agents** (smaller agents who pass students through a bigger "master" agent and share the commission). Edinburgh needs written consent and a list of sub-agents [Known]. Almost all UK universities have signed the Agent Quality Framework pledge, which asks agents to list their sub-agents [Known from snippet, PIE/BUILA]. I found no published commission split between master agent and sub-agent [Unknown: searched PIE, Wonkhe and university agent pages].

**Commission rates paid by Australian universities:**

- The Productivity Commission in 2015 said 11% to 17% of tuition [Known from snippet only].
- Nous Group (Dec 2024, via THE) said Australia pays commissions "up to twice as high" as other destinations, and only 13% of its students are recruited directly by universities. A university may keep as little as A$17,500 of a A$30,000 first-year fee after agent fees and scholarships [Known, THE 18 Dec 2024].
- An example schedule from a Tasmanian secondary school: 25% in year 1, 15% in year 2 and 10% from year 3 [Known from snippet; this is a school, not a university].

**Malaysian portals:**

- **Uni Enrol** earns "10% of a student's first-year tuition fee" from the institution [Known, Vulcan Post Aug 2020. The article is labelled *sponsored*, the figures are the company's own, and they are 6 years old]. It gives students RM300 bursaries for enrolling through it, which is effectively passing some commission back to the student [Known, same source]. A third-party data site describes its business model as "marketplace commission, revenue share" [Known from snippet only, Caplight].
- **EduAdvisor** has a per-application fee (amount not public) plus referral fees and advertising [Known, help-centre].
- **EasyUni** sells listings, verified leads, ad placements and events. A 2013 article mentions commission [Known from snippet].
- One Malaysian private university (MEDIU) uses **flat fees**, e.g. RM300 per student for a short language course [Known from snippet of its agent agreement PDF].

**IDP, the only public accounts** [Known, FY25 results presentation, 28 Aug 2025, which I extracted from the ASX PDF]:

- Student Placement revenue: **A$427.1m**. Of that, "Placements" (commission plus student fees) was **A$367.0m**.
- Placements: **70,076** students, down 29%. Average placement price **A$5,237**, up 15% "from higher client tuition fees, negotiated commission rates, destination mix and increased value-added student services".
- The Asia segment made A$569.8m, about 65% of group revenue.
- **Malaysia is not reported on its own.** The source-country chart shows India 44%, China 14%, Vietnam 5%, then "Rest of Asia 12%", which includes Malaysia [Known].
- At about RM2.8 to the Australian dollar (an exchange rate I assumed and did not look up), A$5,237 is roughly RM14,700 of commission per placed student [estimate].
- FY26 (results 20 Aug 2026): revenue A$795m, placement volumes down 27% and "yield" (revenue per placement) up 11%. IDP also **launched Malaysia as a new *destination*** in FY26, meaning it now recruits foreign students *into* Malaysian universities [Known, FY26 presentation]. So IDP's Malaysian partners now include Malaysian universities themselves (this matters for stream 4).

## 5. Pain points

**Counsellor job ads** (JobStreet, live October 2026; I opened them through Firecrawl):

- **Uni Enrol, "Junior Education Consultant (University Abroad Placement)", RM3,000–4,500.** The ad asks for "a high conversion rate into applications and enrollments" and "To achieve the targets set by the Company in terms of student engagement and enrolment (commission based on target achieved)". It also asks staff to be "Willing to answer priority messages at night from students", "Willing to work overtime during peak period (after exam results)", and to work weekends at Scholar Search events. The candidate profile is "Sales-focused, target-driven". On top of counselling, staff handle "visa applications, accommodation, scholarships, travel arrangements, and any other after-sales services" [Known].
- **Uni Enrol "Senior Education Consultant": RM5,000–6,000.** Same duties, and needs 2+ years of experience [Known].
- **EduAdvisor, "Education Consultant", RM3,000–4,500.** Staff "Research student questions and confirm information with university representatives when needed" and "manage your cases towards agreed application and enrolment targets". The ad asks for "Resourcefulness in finding answers and care in checking their accuracy" [Known].
- Hiring trend: the main study-abroad postings on JobStreet that week were from portals. Fresh graduates are welcome, and no specific degree is needed [Known, EduAdvisor and Uni Enrol ads]. **Experience is thin and staff are new [Guess, from the hiring bar].**
- JobStreet salary insights show average education-consultant pay of about RM3,750–3,800 in KL and Selangor localities [Known, JobStreet salary page].
- **Caseload (how many students each counsellor handles at once):** not stated in any ad I found [Unknown: searched JobStreet, Hiredly, Randstad and MMU careers. Most older ads had expired].

What this suggests [Guess]: the bottleneck is counsellor time and knowledge. Junior staff have to know thousands of courses (Uni Enrol cites 39,000+), check facts with university reps by hand, and convert enquiries to targets during short peaks after exam results. Ready-made research per student could save time, but only where it doesn't divert students away from paying partners.

**Bias and trust:**

- In a BUILA/UKCISA survey, 31% of 105 UK university staff agreed that agents "push students to the institutions where they receive the highest commission rate". 24% of 329 students who had used an agent thought it was biased. The report also says the evidence is "very difficult" to prove either way, and counsellors rarely know the contract terms [Known, THE 30 Mar 2021].
- A Malaysian graduate of Monash, quoted by Study International (Oct 2024), said her agent "talked a little about other universities as well, but it was mainly about Monash" and that "it just didn't feel genuine because I could have looked at other universities" [Known].
- The Australian High Commission in Singapore warns: "If an agent doesn't charge you a fee, they will receive a commission from the education institution and may prefer this institution when advising you" [Known].

**Business pressure:**

- IDP's placement volumes fell 29% in FY25 and 27% in FY26 because of visa policy limits in the four main destination countries, and it cut about 20% of staff [Known, FY25/FY26 presentations].
- Australian universities are under margin pressure from agent costs [Known, Nous via THE].
- Edinburgh's new rule (from 2026) of partial commission for students who already applied [Known] suggests UK universities are tightening what they pay for [Guess].

## 6. How they shortlist today

- **IDP:** counsellors use an "AI-enabled Recommendation Engine" that gives "real-time, personalised course and institution recommendations", with 86% counsellor adoption, plus "Automated course matching". There is also an AI chat advisor ("Navi") that qualifies leads, and "FastLane" offers from partners [Known, FY25 presentation]. **I could not confirm whether the engine recommends only IDP client institutions [Unknown]. It almost certainly favours them, since placements at non-clients earn nothing [Guess, from the footnote quoted in section 7].**
- **Uni Enrol:** "proprietary technology" matches students to "39,000+ courses, 600+ bursaries and scholarships" [Known, JobStreet profile]. In 2020 this was called "Pathway Match": entry requirements for 4,000 courses from 60+ universities [Known, Vulcan Post].
- **EduAdvisor:** a side-by-side comparison of tuition estimates, scholarships and entry requirements. Then a counsellor gives recommendations and the consultant researches the student's questions by hand [Known, help-centre and job ad].
- **MABECS:** "University selection and course matching" from 90 to 100+ UK partners [Known, mabecs.com].
- **Time per shortlist:** not published by anyone [Unknown]. This is a good interview question.
- **Total cost in MYR:** IDP's Malaysia site has a "cost calculator" [Known, idp.com/malaysia]. I found no agent or portal that publishes a whole-degree cost in ringgit per course on a student's list [Unknown: checked IDP, MABECS, AECC, Uni Enrol and EduAdvisor pages].

## 7. Fit with an honest, family-first list

- **Direct conflict [Known plus Guess].** IDP's own definition: placement volume "does not include students placed into non-clients for which we do not receive a commission" [Known]. An agent earns nothing when a student picks a non-partner, so a list that ranks a non-partner first takes money away from the agent [Guess]. This matches competitors.md's "who pays them" test.
- **Where it might help them [Guess]:**
  - (a) As an internal research tool for junior consultants (the job ads show they research and check facts by hand).
  - (b) As a trust signal for agents competing on credibility. MABECS and IDP both advertise trust: IDP reports "94 per cent of students surveyed highly trust IDP".
  - (c) For students headed to destinations or universities the agent doesn't cover, where the agent earns nothing anyway.
  - (d) Reach/Match/Safety (how likely the student is to get in) could reduce wasted applications. UEA claws back commission on early withdrawals, so a bad fit costs the agent too.
- **Where it hurts them [Guess]:**
  - Total-cost-in-MYR and "reasons not to go" make high-commission partners look worse to the family.
  - An any-university list sent out under the agent's name exposes the gap between "best for you" and "pays us".
- **A likely compromise an agent might ask for [Guess]:** a list filtered to partners only, or one with the "reasons not to go" removed. **That would break the product's core promise. Decide in advance whether you would accept it.**
- **Portals:** Uni Enrol already passes some commission back as RM300 bursaries [Known]. That shows it competes on student value, so it might value honesty features more than a traditional agent would [Guess].

## 9. Questions to ask them

Ask about what they did and what they spent. Never ask "would you buy this?"

1. Think of the last student you counselled for the UK or Australia. Walk me through how the list of universities was built. Who built it, how long did it take, and what did you open?
2. How many students does one consultant handle at once in January to March? How many at the busiest point after results?
3. When a student asks about a university you don't partner with, what happened the last time?
4. What did you spend last year on tools, databases or software for course matching (e.g. a CRM, which is customer-tracking software, or an aggregator platform)? Who signed off on it?
5. The last time a student withdrew or was refused a visa, what did it cost you? Were you clawed back?
6. How do new consultants learn the courses? How long until they work on their own? What did the last training cost?
7. Have families ever asked you what the whole degree will cost in ringgit? What did you give them?
8. Have you ever charged a family for anything (premium counselling, guaranteed-offer service)? What did they pay, and how many took it?
9. Who in your firm decides to buy a new service or tool? The last time you bought one, how did that go?
10. (Portals) What share of your revenue is enrolment commission, compared with leads, adverts and events? (Ask; don't assume.)
