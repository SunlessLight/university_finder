## The one difference to lead with

**Everyone who is free is paid by the universities. You are paid by the family.**

Their own websites say so:

- Uni Enrol: its services are "completely free for students because the platform partners
  directly with universities", and "the university pays a service fee".
- MABECS: "Our fees are covered by partner universities as part of our recruitment agreements."
- EasyUni: funded by universities, so the service is free.

That is a fair business, and agents do real work. But it means their list starts from their
partner universities. Yours starts from the student. A university that pays nobody can still be
number one on your list.

Say it as a fact about who pays, not as an accusation. "Agents are biased" starts an argument.
"Agents are paid by universities, I am paid by you" does not.

## Where they are better than you

Put these on the slide yourself. A table where you win every row is a table nobody believes.

- **Agents submit the application and handle the visa, for free.** You hand over a guide and the
  student does the work.
- **Consultants help with essays.** You do not, and your own observation says essays are what
  students fear most. This goes on the "what we build next" slide.
- **Counsellors and agents are people.** A student can ask a follow-up question. Yours is a form.
- **Agents are known names with offices.** You are one student.

## The gap you sit in

| Free | RM50 | RM2,000 and up |
|---|---|---|
| Agents, portals, counsellors, ChatGPT | **You** | Paid consultants |

Nothing I found sits between free and RM2,000. That gap is either an opening or a warning that
families will not pay small amounts for this. The survey question "has your family paid anyone,
and how much?" is how you find out which.

## If the panel asks "why can't an agent just copy this?"

An agent cannot recommend a university that does not pay it without hurting its own income. The
part that is hard to copy is who pays you, not the software.
