# Survey and chats: finding out who actually feels this problem

*For slide 4 (Market Validation) of the LaunchX pitch on 24 October 2026. Written 2026-10-12.*

## What this is testing

Three guesses, from what you noticed about A-level students on 12 October:

- **Guess A:** parents feel this problem and would pay. Students do not.
- **Guess B:** scholarships are the thing students want help with first.
- **Guess C:** essays and the application itself are a bigger fear than choosing a university.

One rule runs through every question below: **ask what people already did, not what they would
do.** "Would you pay for this?" gets a kind answer. "Has your family paid anyone for help so far?"
gets a true one, because the past cannot be polite.

Order of value if exams leave little time: **parent chats, then the counsellor, then the form.**

---

## Part 1: the 2-minute form

Paste these into a new Google Form. Target: 30 or more answers, as many as possible from people
who are not your classmates. Send it to friends of friends, students a year below, other
colleges, and the LaunchX cohort.

**Form title:** How did you (or how are you) choosing a university? 2 minutes

**Form description:**
> I'm a Sunway student building a university research service. I'm trying to learn how people
> actually choose, so there are no right answers. 9 questions, about 2 minutes.

| # | Question | Answer type | Which guess |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where are you right now? | One choice: Pre-university, not applying yet / Applying this year / Already at university / I'm a parent / Other | Sorts the answers |
| 2 | Do you know the person who sent you this form? | One choice: Yes, we're friends or classmates / A little / No, it was forwarded to me | **Separates friends from strangers** |
| 3 | So far, roughly how many hours have you spent researching universities and courses? | One choice: None yet / Under 5 / 5 to 20 / 20 to 50 / More than 50 | How big the effort is |
| 4 | Which of these have you actually used? | Tick all: ChatGPT or Gemini / University websites / Ranking sites / An education agent (IDP, MABECS, etc.) / An education fair / My school or college counsellor / Parents or relatives / Seniors or friends / A paid consultant / Nothing yet | What you compete with |
| 5 | Has your family paid anyone for help with choosing or applying to university? | One choice: No / Yes, under RM100 / Yes, RM100 to RM500 / Yes, RM500 to RM2,000 / Yes, more than RM2,000 / Not sure | **A** (real spending) |
| 6 | Which ONE of these worries you most? | One choice: Finding a scholarship / Knowing the real total cost / Writing essays or a personal statement / Choosing which university or course / Meeting the entry requirements / Visa and paperwork / None of these | **B and C** |
| 7 | Who makes the final decision, and who pays? | Two short choices. Decides: Mostly me / Mostly my parents / Together. Pays: Parents / Scholarship / Loan (PTPTN or other) / A mix | **A** |
| 8 | Think of the last time you got stuck on something about university. What was it, and what did you do? | Short text, optional | Their own words, for slide 2 |
| 9 | I make a personal university list: 8 to 12 universities matched to your grades and budget, with the total cost in ringgit and the scholarships for each. RM50, slots open in November. Leave your WhatsApp number if you want one. Leaving it blank is a useful answer too. | Short text, optional | **Real willingness to pay** |

Under question 9 add this line: *"I'll only use your number to message you about this."* A phone
number is personal data, so keep the response sheet private and out of anything you share.

**Why question 9 matters most.** A person who types their number has done something. A person who
says "I'd pay RM50" has not. The count of numbers left, split by question 2, is your slide-4
evidence. Those people are also your first customers for the pilot test on 10 to 13 November.

**Do not add** "Would you use a service like this?" or "How much would you pay?". Investors
discount both.

## Part 2: parent chats (three to five, 10 minutes each)

The most important part, and the new one. You are finding out whether the parent is the real
customer.

**How to open** (true, and not a sales pitch):
> "I'm a Sunway student building a university research service. I'm trying to understand how
> families actually decide. Can I ask you about your experience for 10 minutes?"

Ask these in order. Mostly listen. Write down their exact words.

1. Where is your child in the process right now?
2. Walk me through what you have done so far to work out where they should study.
3. Who have you talked to? (Agents, education fairs, the school counsellor, relatives.) How did
   that go?
4. Have you paid for anything so far? Consultants, test preparation, application help? Roughly
   how much?
5. What was the hardest thing to find out?
6. Do you have a number for the total cost of the whole degree? Where did it come from?
7. Who decides in the end? What would make you say no to a choice?
8. What are you most worried about getting wrong?

**Then, and only then**, show one page of the sample report on your phone for a minute and ask:

9. "What in this did you not already know?"

**Close with the offer:**
> "I'm opening paid slots in November. RM50 for the list. Should I put you down?"

Record one of three results: **yes and gave a number**, **no**, or **maybe**. Count "maybe" as
no. It usually is.

**Three things not to do:**

- Do not describe your product before question 8. Once they know what you sell, they answer to be
  nice.
- Do not ask "would you...". If you catch yourself, change it to "have you...".
- Do not fill a silence. People say the true thing after the pause.

## Part 3: students who already received a list (two or three chats)

One question matters most, because it tests your point that students "don't know what they don't
know":

1. **"What did you not know before you saw it?"**

If they are happy to keep talking:

2. What did you do with it afterwards? Did you open it again?
3. Did you show it to anyone? Who?
4. Which part did you actually read?
5. Did it change anything? A university added or dropped?

An answer to question 1 that names a specific fact ("I didn't know the visa needs RM X in the
bank") is a quote for slide 4. "It was very detailed" is a compliment and tells you nothing.

## Part 4: one chat with a Sunway College counsellor or the placement office (10 minutes)

They see hundreds of students, so they can confirm or correct your five observations in one
conversation. **Do not pitch.** You are there to learn.

1. What do students most often come to you confused about?
2. What do you wish they knew earlier?
3. When in the year do they start asking?
4. Roughly what share go abroad, to a local private university, or to a public one?
5. What do parents ask you?
6. Which outside services do students use? (Agents, consultants.) Do any of them pay?
7. About how many A-level students are there in one intake?

Questions 4 and 7 also give you real numbers for the market-size slide, to replace my estimates
in [market_sizing.md](market_sizing.md).

## Part 6: brief for a helper

Give this to anyone helping, so they can do it without you.

> **What it's for:** Evan is testing whether people need help choosing a university. He pitches
> on 24 October.
>
> **Your task:** get 10 form answers from people who do not know Evan. Juniors, friends at other
> colleges, cousins, anyone choosing a university this year or last. Send them the link with one
> line of your own.
>
> **Two rules:**
> 1. Do not explain or sell the product. If someone asks, say "it's a university research thing,
>    the form explains".
> 2. Do not fill it in for anyone and do not nudge the answers. An honest "no" helps more than a
>    polite "yes".
>
> **If you sit in on a parent chat:** your job is to write down what the parent says, in their
> words. Evan asks the questions.
>
> **Deadline:** 20 October, so the answers can go into the slides.

## Part 7: what to count on 21 October

Give me the form's response sheet (or just these counts) and I put them into the slides. With
fewer than 30 answers, report counts, not percentages: "7 of 22", not "32%".

| What to count | Tests | Goes on |
|---|---|---|
| Total answers, and how many were from strangers (question 2) | How far to trust the rest | Slide 4 |
| WhatsApp numbers left: friends vs strangers (question 9) | Real willingness to pay | Slide 4 |
| Families who have already paid for help, and how much (question 5) | Guess A, and what price is normal | Slides 4 and 8 |
| Top worry (question 6) | Guesses B and C | Slides 2 and 3 |
| Who decides and who pays (question 7) | Guess A | Slide 7 |
| Parents: how many said yes to RM50 and gave a number | Guess A | Slide 4 |
| What tools people used (question 4) | Who you really compete with | Slide 6 |

If all three guesses turn out wrong, that is still a slide. "Here is what I tested and what I
learned" is exactly what a stress test asks for.
