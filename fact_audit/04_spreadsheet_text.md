# 04. Text the student reads in the spreadsheet (glossary tab, column explanations, fixed wording)

*Fact audit, 2026-10-28. Written for Evan (no technical background). Labels: G guess, A advice,
Y yapping, J unlabelled judgment, L loophole. Severity: High reaches a student, Medium could,
Low internal.*

## What this part makes
The student's spreadsheet (the "master list": one row per university, 35 columns) comes with a
second tab, the **Glossary**: a two-column list of "Term / What it means" for the jargon the list
uses (ED, CSS Profile, ABET, MQA…). The glossary text is not researched per student: it is a fixed
dictionary of ~190 hand-written definitions inside `tools/apply_glossary.py` (think of it as a
pre-printed phrasebook). `tools/build_glossary_sheet.py` scans the student's list, picks out the
terms it finds, and copies their phrasebook definitions into the tab. The same phrasebook also
feeds the "Key terms" box in the apply guides. The sample pack (`business/sales/sample_pack/`) is
one real list with the name removed, plus its glossary tab; it is what prospects and the booth see.
There is no separate "what each column means" sheet: the glossary covers acronyms only.

## Who sees it
- **Students:** their `master_list.csv` + `glossary.csv` (Google Sheets), and the "Key terms"
  section of every apply guide.
- **The public:** `sample_master_list.csv` and `sample_glossary.csv` (sent to prospects, shown at
  the booth). Audited here in their current working-tree state (both files are modified, not
  committed), where the five US "Well above" cells already read "Not published".
- **Evan:** `business/sales/sample_pack/README.md`.

## What is already solid
- The glossary tab only lists terms the list actually uses (`present_terms`), so it never pads
  the student's sheet with unrelated jargon.
- `check_master_list.py` already catches swap-able jargon (`PLAIN_ALTERNATIVES`) and the
  grades-vs-likelihood contradiction, so a Reach row whose grades clear the bar must say why
  ("Reach (very selective)", "Reach (test + interview)"), which it does on every such sample row.
- In the current sample, the five US "Well above" cells read "Not published", and Imperial's
  English cell now states the real rule ("No waiver for schooling in English").
- Many cells already say what was not found, in plain words: "Monash publishes no award
  count/rate", "no fixed minimum published", "CSC does not publish per-university award rates",
  "no conversion rate is published", and cite where a claim comes from ("MIT FAQ", "per
  Rose-Hulman's own pages", "Northeastern page").
- The IELTS definition ends "Each sets its own rule" instead of promising a waiver.
- The `Approx total (MYR)` column says it is approximate in its name.
